### 2. The Core Corporate Problem
Insurance firms frequently fail or suffer severe ratings downgrades during sudden healthcare crises not because their long-term models are wrong, but because they experience a catastrophic **short-term working capital liquidity crunch**. During an epidemic, claims volume surges exponentially while a high percentage of claims remain frozen under regulatory review or processing bottlenecks. If the company does not hold the exact right cash reserve, it faces structural insolvency.

### 3. The Central Actuarial Research Questions
 **Research Question 1 (Macro Risk):** *What is the total financial claims shock wave generated by an epidemic surge across an insured pool of 100,000 members, and at what exact timestamp does the peak daily liquidity strain threaten our cash reserves?*
 **Research Question 2 (Stochastic Volatility):** *Accounting for random fluctuations in virus mutations and extreme right-skewed ICU medical billing spikes, what is the precise 95% Value-at-Risk (VaR) and Tail Value-at-Risk (TVaR) boundary required to preserve corporate solvency?*
 **Research Question 3 (Micro Operational Risk):** *How does the presence of right-censored pending claims impact cash flow clearance velocity, and does systemic processing latency vary significantly across commercial vs. public insurance sectors?*

##  How I Achieved the Objectives Using SQL & Python

To answer these research questions, I built a modular 4-phase data architecture:

###  Phase 1: Data Engineering & Relational Profiling (`scripts/01_data_import.py`)
 **Tooling Used:** Python `pandas`, `sqlite3` engine, and SQL relational query syntax.
 **Execution:** Automated the ingestion of raw billing records (`claim_data.csv`). I programmatically lowercased attributes and removed whitespaces to enforce relational database constraints.
 **SQL Profiling Insight:** I executed optimized `GROUP BY` extraction queries to isolate historical base costs. Across all insurance sectors (Commercial, Medicare, Medicaid, Self-Pay), the baseline payout hovered tightly around **\$200.75 per claim**, establishing our clean underwriting baseline.

###  Phase 2: Macro Epidemic Financial Shock Simulation (`scripts/02_pandemic_simulation.py`)
 **Tooling Used:** Python `scipy.integrate.odeint`, `numpy`, and mathematical calculus.
 **Execution:** Instead of relying on lazy static trend lines, I coded an **SEIR (Susceptible, Exposed, Infected, Recovered) Differential Equation model**. I integrated the disease transmission rates over a 120-day horizon.
 **Actuarial Application:** Multiplied the daily infection curves against our database cost metrics loaded with a 2.5x diagnostic severity multiplier. The model successfully exposed a **\$2,338,148.34 total financial shock wave**, pinpointing the exact peak crisis window on **Day 34 (\$142.5k drain in a single day)**.

### 📈 Phase 3: Actuarial Survival Analysis & Censoring (`scripts/03_survival_analysis.py`)
 **Tooling Used:** Python `lifelines` engine, `KaplanMeierFitter`, and `matplotlib`.
 **Execution:** Accounted for real-world insurance data boundaries by building a **Kaplan-Meier Survival model** to track "Time-to-Adjudication." This explicitly resolved **right-censored data** (claims still frozen in 'Under Review' status at the end of the observation frame).
 **Visual Insight:** Proved that **33.8% of claims remain gridlocked past Day 60**, adding severe operational liquidity delays onto the underwriting crisis. The model also isolated performance variance shifts, showing **Medicaid clears 4 days faster (34-day median)** than commercial alternatives (38-day median).

###  Phase 4: Stochastic Monte Carlo Simulation (`scripts/04_stochastic_monte_carlo.py`)
 **Tooling Used:** Python statistical distribution sampling and random iterative loops.
 **Execution:** Evolved the pipeline into a rigorous stochastic risk environment by executing **1,000 parallel simulated pandemics**. I drew transmission rates from a Normal distribution and medical cost severity loads from a skewed Lognormal distribution to capture extreme tail volatility.
 **Solvency Cushion Output:** Derived the definitive **95% Actuarial Value-at-Risk (VaR) at \$3,046,488.85** and **Tail Value-at-Risk (TVaR) at \$3,239,669.43**, shifting our corporate reserve recommendations from baseline guesswork to proven probability boundaries.
